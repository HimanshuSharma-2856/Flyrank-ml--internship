## Work / Case Study

### FlyRank content-archetype analysis

#### The problem

A large page portfolio is difficult to review consistently. An editor can see individual pages, but a page-by-page process makes it hard to compare similar work, choose the right checklist, or decide where limited review time should go first.

My question was: **Which structural content archetypes exist in the starter inventory, and can their profiles help an editor decide what kind of review or measurement each page needs first?**

The person who acts on this work is a content strategist or editor. The cost of a wrong call is practical: time may go to a low-value group, the wrong checklist may be applied, or a page needing attention may be missed.

#### What I did

I chose the Structured Content Archetype Clustering lane. I treated content type, search intent, word count, content age, and update recency as observable structure that might reveal recurring page profiles.

I documented the data path and checked the starter slice before making the case for the lane: 30,000 rows, 32 pseudonymized clients, and more than one content type. I kept pseudonymous IDs out of the features and did not use the starter trend label as a cluster feature. A simple rule table remains the baseline; clustering earns its place only if its groups are stable, interpretable, and useful for a different review decision.

The output is not an automatic rewrite list. It is a small set of human-readable profiles, uncertainty checks, and a suggested next action for each profile. The safe workflow is to sample an archetype, inspect the pages, and then decide whether content, metadata, internal linking, or measurement needs attention.

#### What came of it

The project turned an abstract clustering idea into a bounded editorial decision aid. It made the useful claim narrower: structural differences can be measured and organized into profiles that help an editor compare pages and choose a review approach.

That is descriptive decision support, not proof that a cluster causes better rankings, traffic, clicks, or conversions. The starter data is a trailing 90-day snapshot, and this lane has no ground-truth labels. Any relationship with search or engagement metrics would be directional and would need volume floors and client-aware checks.
